# Sumter County Data Center Community Research Report

## Start here

A community research report on the proposed Sumter County data center. The short guides collect evidence, identify missing records and give residents specific questions to raise with commissioners.

No complete public project application or engineering package has been located. The large planning numbers in this report are **scenarios**, not forecasts for this site.

A signed [Economic Development Agreement dated July 1, 2026](09-development-agreement.md) between the Americus-Sumter Payroll Development Authority and Liberty GA USA LLC names the company, parcel, construction phases and operating conditions. It gives no figures for power, water or noise. Other planning materials identify a **400 MW IT load**: an unexecuted purchase agreement gives Phase 1 as 100 MW, and the September 8 community presentation describes four 100 MW buildings.

## Public meetings

Two public meetings took place in July 2026: the **Sumter County Board of Commissioners** (500 West Lamar Street, Americus; confirmed by the county calendar) and the **Americus City Council** (Russell Thomas Jr. Public Safety Building, Lee Street). The council discussed a draft data-center ordinance - industrial-district siting with special approval, a 250-foot residential buffer, and a 70-decibel sound cap - as reported by the [Americus Times-Recorder on July 17, 2026](https://americustimesrecorder.com/2026/07/17/americus-city-council-discusses-data-center-ordinance/). Neither the city nor the county has adopted an ordinance governing data centers; that gap is what the [joint moratorium resolution](/research/moratorium-resolution.pdf) asks for time to fill.

Residents held a meeting on August 4, 2026 at the Lake Blackshear Regional Library. This site has the [recording, a written summary and the slides](/meetings/2026-08-04-data-centers-are-coming/). The summary attributes claims to the speakers; the Field Desk has not verified their figures.

The [public meeting calendar](/meetings/) lists confirmed commission, council and Development Authority meetings, including the room, time and published public-comment rules. Each date was copied from the body's posted calendar. You can also check the [city's agenda portal](https://www.americusga.gov/) or call 229-924-4411.

Use these guides to prepare questions about water, sound, air pollution, electricity, public costs and enforceable conditions. Check posted agendas and last-minute notices before attending.

## What is known

- Americus municipal water [comes from groundwater wells](https://cms3.revize.com/revize/americusga/2024%20Americus%20CCR.pdf).
- The city holds [groundwater-withdrawal permit 129-0001](https://epd.georgia.gov/watershed-protection-branch-lists).
- The permit lists 3.75 million gallons per day (MGD) as the annual-average limit and 4.2 MGD as the monthly-average limit.
- The [Development Authority advertises](https://www.selectsumter.com/sites-buildings) approximately 2.5 MGD of average city water consumption and a 7 MGD water-plant capacity.
- The [signed development agreement](09-development-agreement.md) places the project on about 102 acres on Swett Avenue, tax parcel 64-17, and names **Liberty GA USA LLC** as the developer.
- Planning materials describe the four-building campus as a **400 MW IT load**, with 100 MW in Phase 1. The signed agreement itself does not state a megawatt figure.
- **The land has been conveyed.** The Authority deeded the tract to Liberty GA USA LLC on **July 22, 2026**, recorded the next day in Deed Book 1806, Pages 746-748.
- **Most of the site is wetland.** A 2023 delineation found [61.47 of 103.91 acres, 59%, are wetland](14-what-can-actually-be-built.md), which cannot be built on without a federal permit. No permit application has been located.
- That agreement conveys the land, agreed to be worth $469,000, to Liberty for **$1.00**, in exchange for four construction phases through December 2033, a 30-year operating covenant, and at least 150 jobs at an average wage of $80,000.
- The PDA agreement bars private water wells and open-loop cooling and limits backup generators to emergencies. The city ordinance contains no water-use standard, so those contract terms are not city zoning requirements and are not enforceable by the city through the ordinance.
- The people behind the proposal are **Tony and Paul Di Benedetto**, of Toronto. Tony Di Benedetto's firm, Launch Capital Inc., lists Liberty Data Centers first in its published portfolio, and both men answered for Liberty at the [community meeting of August 4](/meetings/2026-08-04-data-centers-are-coming/) - the company's first public appearance in Americus. See [who is behind the proposal](10-liberty-data-centers.md).

## What is not known publicly

- The end user or tenant of the compute. Liberty said on August 4 that it has a customer; it did not name one
- Who the officers, directors, and owners of Liberty GA USA LLC are, and who signed for it on July 1. The company publishes no name, and no corporate filing has been retrieved
- Total facility demand above the planned 400 MW IT load
- Final cooling and heat-rejection design, and whether it is evaporative
- Average and peak water demand
- Generator count, models, fuel, and allowed operating hours
- Utility service commitments and upgrade costs
- Any tax abatement, bond, or payment-in-lieu-of-taxes agreement

## How to read the labels

- **Verified fact** - supported by a named source.
- **Scenario** - arithmetic showing scale, not a prediction for Americus.
- **Unknown** - project-specific information that was not located publicly.
- **Recommendation** - a proposed safeguard or policy choice, not an existing legal requirement.
- **Caution** - a claim that is incomplete, weakly sourced, or easy to misstate.

## Editorial standard

The report distinguishes sourced facts from scenarios, unknowns and recommendations. Undocumented local claims are identified as such. The verification note lists records and answers still needed before commissioners act.
